Caricature drawing uses selective exaggeration to reveal what makes a person or subject recognisable. It is not simply about making features larger; it is about identifying the details that carry personality, expression, and presence.

THE ESSENTIALS

What gives a caricature
its character?

Strong caricature begins with observation. The artist studies proportions and expressions, then decides which features deserve emphasis and which details can be simplified.

01

Recognition

Keep the distinctive qualities that make the subject identifiable, even when the proportions are intentionally altered.

02

Exaggeration

Push selected shapes, gestures, or expressions with purpose. A small, deliberate change can communicate more than exaggerating everything.

03

Simplification

Remove visual noise so the important forms remain clear. Every line should support the overall impression.

04

Expression

Pay attention to the eyes, mouth, posture, and the relationships between them. Character often lives in those subtle connections.

A PRACTICE TO TRY

Look first. Draw second.

Choose a face, spend a moment observing its most distinctive features, and make a quick study using only a few confident lines. Focus on capturing the impression rather than chasing photographic accuracy.
